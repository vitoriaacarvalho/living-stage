# 9.1 — 3D stage and ballerinas

Requirements: **L1** (perspective stage with floor, wings, guides and legs), **L2** (shaded ballerinas in the dancer color, skin and hair from the id), **L3** (soft floor shadow, wing dancers at 80%; the selected ring moves to 2.4), **L4** (labels above every figure), **L5** (3D by default, 2D one tap away), **L6** (60 ballerinas smooth on an iPad), and the 3D halves of **C1** and **C5**.

Done when: cast dancers stand as 3D ballerinas on the perspective stage, colored by dancer or role, with readable labels; one tap switches to the 2D plan.

Screens: `Stage3D`, `DanceEditorIPad` (load the 3D skills listed in `CLAUDE.local.md` and the `living-stage-design` skill).

## Decisions

- **Real WebGL, not the design's drawing.** The design's `Stage3D` is one SVG that fakes depth; the plan's Three.js through React Three Fiber and drei wins. The design also gives Odette and the prince their own poses; the plan's one shared pose wins.
- **One React group per ballerina first** (shared geometries, one material per color), then measured with 60 ballerinas on an iPad within this plan. If it is not smooth, the parts move to instanced meshes (one per part, a second set for the wings) before 9.1 is done.
- **Labels are drei `<Html>`**: real DOM over the canvas, with the same type and tokens as the 2D labels and the same name / role / both modes. Being DOM, they always sit above every figure (L4).
- **A fixed audience camera**, centred and slightly raised, framed so the floor and both wings fit at any size. No orbit or zoom, so dragging in 2.4 stays predictable.
- **An open spot is a dashed ring on the floor with its label**, no ballerina, matching the 2D plan.
- **Every dance opens in 3D.** The "2D plan / 3D stage" `SegmentedControl` sits in the view controls row and is not saved.
- **The selected ring (L3) moves to 2.4**, since nothing can be selected before dragging and selection.
- **Cheap by default:** a blurred disc under each ballerina instead of shadow maps; one hemisphere and one directional light; pixel ratio capped at 2; the scene renders on demand (`frameloop="demand"`), not every frame.
- **The 3D code is lazy-loaded**, so Three.js downloads only when a 3D stage is shown.
- **Skin and hair come from a hash of the dancer's id** (`skin-1`–`skin-4`, `hair-1`–`hair-3`), in `packages/shared`, so they match everywhere.
- **Tests cover the pure parts** (stage units to metres, skin and hair, label text); jsdom has no WebGL, so editor tests replace `Stage3D` with a stub and the look is checked in the browser.
